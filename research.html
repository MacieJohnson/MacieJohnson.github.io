Research Statement

“Mining of the periodic table and classic organic literature are two avenues that are likely to bear interesting fruit.”

-Carolyn Bertozzi

Bertozzi describes certain avenues of research as likely to "bear interesting fruit." I posit the relationship between light exposure and myopia is one of those avenues. Despite a growing body of evidence, results still differ by species and time of day, which leaves room for new discoveries.

Myopia, or nearsightedness, results from excessive axial elongation of the eye, and research in Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science indicates that the eye uses environmental photic cues to regulate that growth. Recent studies have shown that the wavelength and timing of light can modulate axial length in humans and animals, though results differ depending on the species and the time of day. I am interested in discovering what, if any, causal relationship exists between the wavelength and timing of diurnal light exposure and ocular growth in children. Further research in this field has the potential to yield preventative light-based strategies for myopia, an advancement compared to current optical corrections, which compensate for refractive error only after the onset of myopia.
